# Futures Historical Bars, Statistics and Tick Aggregation Architecture

## Overview

Initiative 220 built and proved the futures tick pipeline, but on two free-credit files, and it has never bought anything. Production holds ES from 2024-08-30 to 2024-12-31 (64 sessions) and GC from 2024-11-04 to 2024-12-31 (41 sessions), both at `tbbo`. That is enough for the engine and the UI to build and test on. This initiative makes that data usable: bars of N trades at any N, time bars at any size, compact raw ticks, checks that say whether what is stored is complete, and storage for provider bars and statistics so they can be used when they are held.

**Priority (journal 20261115 ADR, which replaces the bar-source rule this document was first written around).** Ticks are the primary futures data. Provider bars and statistics are optional extras: used where held, never required. Nothing is bought because it is available; a purchase needs a use named by the PM. Provider bars extend coverage far beyond the ticks (back to 2010 with the Standard plan), and that reach is wanted when the plan starts; it is not something to buy ahead of a use.

The shape of what Databento sells decides the design. Verified on the pricing page on 2026-10-28, the Standard plan ($199/month) includes `ohlcv-1s/1m/1h/1d`, `definition`, `statistics` (open interest, settlement) and `status` for 16+ years, and `trades`, `tbbo`, `bbo` and `mbp-1` for the last 12 months only. Ticks therefore cover a rolling year plus the files we already hold. Bars and statistics cover the whole history, so they are what a later look further back would use. None of that is needed now.

**Scope:** the bar and statistics schemas as archive units with their own storage; the open-interest roll rule; tick bars at a user-chosen N and compact raw-tick serving (journal 20261101 ADR); completeness checks for every futures tier; serving of the new data; and a purchase path with spend limits, ready for a first small purchase when the PM names one. It is the part of 280's original scope that does not depend on a live feed, moved here on 2026-10-28.

**Motivation:** the primary research target is futures, and the engine and the UI need usable futures data to keep developing while the Databento subscription is deliberately delayed (it is expensive). The ticks already held are that data. What was missing: bars of N trades, compact tick serving, a way for `mt data health` and `accounting` to say whether a futures range is complete, and open interest, which 220 reserved for later.

## Design Goals

- **Ticks are the primary data** — Ticks are what the project exists to hold, and they can answer every futures question for the ranges we have. Raw ticks and bars of N trades at any N always come from ticks, with identical bars for the chart and the engine.
- **One time-bar read path that says where its bars came from** — A consumer asks for time bars of any size. Where we hold provider bars that can make that size (the same size, or a finer one added up), they are used, because reading them is faster. Where we do not, the bars are cut from ticks, which is slower. The response names the source.
- **Every tier answers "what do I have, and is it complete"** — Ticks, bars and statistics each get a completeness verdict against the CME calendar, in the same surfaces operators already read.
- **A purchase path with limits, ready to be proven** — Spend limits, a free estimate, a reconciliation against Databento's own charge and one purchase runner. It has not spent real money in production. The first purchase the PM names goes through it as a small one.
- **Open interest available as a roll rule** — The rule 220 evaluated and deferred, built on stored `statistics`.

## Architectural Principles

- **Provider bars are a tier of their own** — `ohlcv-1m`, `ohlcv-1s` and `statistics` are new archive schemas, each projected into its own table with contract identity. Never into `minute_ohlcv` (equities, symbol-keyed) and never into the tick hypertable. This repeats 220's rule that a futures contract is the instrument and a continuous series is a rule.
- **File-first, as in 220** — Raw DBN is archived and recorded in the manifest before anything is projected. Acquisition and ingest stay separate and idempotent; a database rebuilt from the archive holds the same projection.
- **Cost is a first-class input, never a surprise** — Every request that could cost money is preceded by an estimate and passes the spend guard, and the guard counts marginal spend only: a pull the plan includes is marked so, and costs the guard nothing. The PM sets the ceilings.
- **A tier ships with its completeness check** — The slice that adds a schema also extends `status`, `coverage`, `health` and `accounting` for it, and is not done until they can say whether its range is complete. A tier that cannot be verified is not delivered.
- **Completeness comes from the manifest and the ledger, never from an aggregate** — Same rule as 220. A continuous aggregate answers a read; it never answers "what do I have", and nothing it derives informs acquisition (journal 20260725).
- **Never splice sources silently** — A bars response names the source it used (Technical Considerations, "Which source serves bars"). Mixing is handled by saying so, never by refusing to serve. Where both ticks and provider bars are held, their agreement is measured by an automatic background check.
- **Everything is built and tested before it costs money** — Code runs against real DBN files (the local files and the DBN project's samples), never invented fixtures. Real spending starts with a bounded test purchase.
- **Every feature answers the API question** — Whether it belongs in `/api/v1/futures/*`, with human and AI documentation, is decided per feature, not at the end.

## Current State

- **Ticks:** the pipeline works end to end (adapter, archive, manifest, ingest, calendars, roll rules for calendar and volume, `/api/v1/futures/*`). ES and GC are configured at `tbbo`, measured at 28.6 bytes per row compressed and about 4.7 GB per year for both. The pass has only run on the two free-credit files; the spend ceiling is $0.50.
- **Bars:** the futures bars routes compute bars at read time. `read_bars` runs `time_bucket` over one session's `tick_trade` rows, so bars exist only where ticks do, and a range read is one bounded scan per session. There are no continuous aggregates over `tick_trade`, which is an integer-nanosecond hypertable.
- **Schemas:** the schema enum knows `trades`, `tbbo`, `mbp-1` and `definition`. `ohlcv-*` and `statistics` cannot be archived today.
- **Open interest:** evaluated in 220 and deferred. For ES, Databento's `.n.0` matched `.v.0` on all 11 rolls from 2023 to 2025; GC differs.
- **Checks:** `mt data health` reports the tick database's state and retention. `accounting` and the status coverage aggregates map only `minute_ohlcv` and `daily_ohlcv`. Tick completeness is a manual raw-count proof (`mt data tick coverage`).
- **As of 2026-11-15:** production holds 27.7 million ES and 4.0 million GC tick records and no provider bars or statistics. Nothing has been bought into production; the only real spend is $0.06 on slice 303's test window. Slice 306 made time bars provider-only, so the time-bar routes return nothing until the 20261115 rule is built.
- **Consumers:** trading-ui plots equities from the API. Neither it nor manta-engine calls the futures routes yet.

## Envisioned State

Coverage is everything we hold, not only where we hold ticks.

- **Where we hold ticks** (a few instruments and a limited window, because ticks are expensive to buy, store and back up): raw ticks in the compact encoding, bars of N trades at any N, and time bars at any size.
- **Where we hold provider bars and no ticks** (with the Standard plan, 1-second and 1-minute bars reach back as far as 2010 at no extra cost; covering that span with ticks would cost thousands of dollars and need new storage): time bars at every size those bars can make. There are no raw ticks and no N-trade bars there, and the response says why.
- **Where we hold both**: time bars come from the provider bars, and their agreement with the ticks is checked in the background.

The archive holds `ohlcv-1m`, `ohlcv-1s` and `statistics` units once they come with the subscription or the PM names a purchase, each recorded in the manifest with its cost, ingested into its own table, and counted by the same completeness vocabulary 220 uses. Until then the ticks already held are the whole of the futures data, and they are enough for the engine and the UI to build and test on. A single bars read names its source. `mt data tick coverage`, `health`, `accounting` and `overview` report every futures tier against the CME session calendar, and a gap is a named finding, not a surprise in a backtest. The open-interest roll rule reads stored `statistics` where they are held. The purchase path is ready and limited; it is proven the first time the PM names a purchase.

## Technical Considerations

- **What a "day" is** — Two different days run through this work, and they must not be mixed. The archive unit is one UTC calendar day of a request, because that is the grain Databento splits files at. The trading day is the CME session: it opens in the evening of the previous calendar date, runs through the daily maintenance break (17:00–18:00 ET in the current era; the Globex close was earlier before about September 2015, so slice 301 dates regular hours) and closes the next afternoon, with the Sunday-evening open and the holiday exceptions of the 221 session model. A session therefore spans two UTC dates and is covered by two units, and a unit can hold the end of one session and the start of the next. Coverage, the ingest ledger, the `1d` bar and every completeness verdict are per session; only acquisition and the manifest are per UTC day.
- **Which source serves bars** — Decided in the journal's 20261115 ADR. For the range asked for, stored provider bars are served where we hold them, because reading them is faster than scanning ticks; ticks are cut into bars everywhere else. A coarser size is added up from a finer stored size on the 221 session model, with intraday buckets anchored at the session open (issue #47). A time bar may be any whole number of seconds, minutes or hours, or the session day; there is no fixed menu. Where there are neither ticks nor stored bars there are no bars, and the response says so. Every response names the source it used. Provider `ohlcv-1h` and `ohlcv-1d` are not served as our 1h and 1d: they are bucketed by their own clock, not the CME session, so serving them would silently change what a daily bar means (PM, 2026-10-28). Stored aggregates of bars are not built now; they are an option if a real read is too slow.
- **What 1-second bars are for** — A 1-second bar is served like any other size: from stored `ohlcv-1s` where held, from ticks otherwise. Provider `ohlcv-1s` is not stored today. It comes with the Standard plan, which we will take when realtime work starts and which includes it for the whole history, so it is not something we expect to pay for separately. It is stored then, for the ranges and instruments the PM chooses; ES and GC over all history is about 17 GB. A purchase at list price would only arise for something the plan does not include, and needs a use named by the PM. A bars-only product would need an explicit "no ticks wanted" marker on its universe entry (302 D5).
- **Tick bars and raw ticks** — Decided in the journal's 20261101 ADR, which is the full record. Ticks (`tbbo`/`trades`) are served two ways. **Tick bars:** N trades per bar, N any positive integer set per request. One tick is one trade record. The count restarts at each session open. The last bar of a session is kept, and is marked partial when it holds fewer than N trades (a session whose trade count is a multiple of N ends with a full bar; slice 308 D2). A bar carries open and close times, OHLC, volume, trade count, buy and sell volume by aggressor side, the bid/ask at close, and its identity (session date, index within session). **Raw ticks:** a compact columnar encoding (integer nanosecond timestamps as deltas, integer prices, symbol and session once per segment). The live stream (280) will use the same encoding, with a stream key (`ts_event`, `sequence`, `sequence_ordinal`) on every message. The rollup is one incremental module (one trade in; closed bars and the current partial bar out). History runs it in a loop, there is no SQL second implementation, and it is checked against fixtures from real `tbbo` sessions. The chart and the engine get identical bars because trading-data cuts them and no consumer re-implements the rule. Server-side cutting is known not to scale past a handful of clients. At scale, a per-client engine runs the same module on its own feed. Time bars cut from ticks are computed at read time; stored aggregates are not built now (journal 20261115 ADR).
- **Parity between tiers** — Provider bars are trade-only bars per second or minute. Tick-derived bars from `tbbo` trades should match them for the same instrument and interval. Parity is measured on a window where we hold both, not assumed, by an automatic background check and not by a command an operator types. Production holds no provider bars, so there is nothing to compare yet.
- **Completeness of sparse bars** — An `ohlcv-1s` bar exists only for a second with a trade, so a row count per session proves nothing, and a quiet overnight session is not a hole. The ledger proves a load, not that the market data is whole. So bar completeness has two parts, both committed here. **Load-complete:** the unit is verified and its counts and first and last event time match the manifest. **Content-complete:** where both tiers exist, a session's `ohlcv-1m` volume does not exceed `statistics` cleared volume (it may fall below it: cleared volume includes block, exchange-for-physical and exchange-for-related-position trades that bars and ticks do not carry; more traded than cleared, or none traded against cleared volume, is the finding; amended per the 303 slice review), `ohlcv-1s` volume summed to minutes agrees with `ohlcv-1m`, and a provider day condition of `degraded` or `missing` is shown as such. A session that fails either part is a named finding. Where only load-completeness is possible the surface says so.
- **Statistics** — Contents (settlement, cleared volume, open interest, session statistics) and publication timing (CME publishes session D's statistics during D + 1, pinned in 220's constants) set when a session's statistics are complete. Because they arrive after the session, a statistics record's event time falls in a later UTC-day unit than the session it describes. The session is assigned from the record's reference time and trading date, never its event time, so the per-unit session ledger cannot be applied to statistics as it is to ticks. Revisions are a second, record-level supersession alongside the manifest's unit-level one: a correction can arrive in a later unit, and the winner is chosen by the record's update action and receive time. Both rules are confirmed against the DBN statistics record definition at slice design, and the roll rule reads only resolved values.
- **Open-interest roll** — Needs per-contract daily open interest from `statistics`, aligned to the 221 session model. ES `.n.0` matched `.v.0` on all 11 rolls; GC does not, so GC is where the rule earns its place. The rule vocabulary must match Databento's continuous-symbol conventions closely enough that a request in either resolves to the same contract on the same day.
- **Purchase path** — Slice 304 built the spend-limit script (the PM sets the per-run limit and the 30-day cap), a reconciliation of each request's estimate and recorded cost against Databento's own charge, and one purchase runner that estimates, buys, ingests and writes evidence. The purchase itself never ran in production. The first purchase the PM names runs through this path as a small one, usage-billed if it is outside a subscription.
- **The rolling tick window** — In-plan `trades`/`tbbo` cover only the last 12 months, so each day without a subscription moves the oldest in-plan day out of the plan. This is a known cost of delay (PM, 2026-10-28), not a blocker. Selected older history can be bought outside the plan as budget allows.
- **Serving** — `/api/v1/futures/products/{product}/bars` and the contract routes serve time bars under the source rule above. Responses are gzipped when the client accepts it (level 1, encoded off the event loop). The response names its source and echoes every parameter the server applied, defaults included (issue #77), and the change is documented in `docs/api/reference.md`, `agents.md` and the OpenAPI file. Open interest and settlement need routes or an explicit decision not to serve them.
- **Surface coverage** — `COVERAGE_SOURCE_TABLE` and the status coverage logic map only minute and daily tables; tick and the new tiers need their own definition of "source edge" and "caught up". The slice that adds a tier extends these surfaces in place; no parallel set.
- **Cost accounting** — The 30-day spend guard sums every manifest request plus every job the provider lists that the manifest lacks, and the request row has no way to say a pull is plan-included. Recording plan-included pulls at list price would stop the history acquisition at the first large pull (ES and GC `ohlcv-1s` alone is $1,111 against a $0.50 ceiling), and raising the ceilings would stop the guard protecting the usage-billed purchases outside the plan. So each request carries a plan-inclusion marker, set from one table (schema to included history) kept in configuration, and the guard counts marginal spend only. List-price estimates stay on the row for information. Invoice reconciliation applies to usage-billed requests.
- **The plan-inclusion marker must not be a way round the spend guard** — As built, the marker is set from a table in the code, so an agent that decided a purchase was important could edit the table, mark the purchase as included, and the guard would count it as $0. Anything that lives in the repository can be changed by an agent, tests included. The controls that hold are the ones outside an agent's reach, and the design rests on three of them (PM, 2026-11-15):
  1. **The hard stop is Databento's own account spending limit**, set by the PM on Databento's site. No agent can change it. Our guard is the early warning, not the last line.
  2. **Our own claim of "included" is never trusted alone.** Every request is priced by Databento before it is made. A request we marked as included that Databento quotes above $0 is refused. Unverified until a subscription exists: whether Databento's quote reads $0 for plan-included data. If it does not, this control does not work and the other two carry the load.
  3. **Money that slipped through is a failing health check.** The reconciliation compares what the guard counted with what Databento charged; a request counted as $0 that Databento charged for is a `FAIL` in `mt data health`, not a line in a report.
  The list of what the plan includes moves out of the code into the root-owned production environment file, beside the spend limits, so changing it takes the PM's root command. This is its own piece of work, after the time-bar change.
- **Generalising the archive beyond tick tiers** — Adding `ohlcv-*` and `statistics` to the schema enum is not enough: the enum feeds tick-tier logic. Tier order drives the supersession rank, the stored-tier set decides what ingest selects, the preflight judges a tier-plus-definition bundle, and the ingest copy is written for `tick_trade`. The architecture's position: the archive and manifest become schema-family aware, with families for tick tiers, bars, statistics and definitions; supersession applies within a family only (a bar unit never supersedes a `tbbo` unit); ingest selects a projector per schema from one registry, replacing the single hard-wired writer; and the bundle rule stays a tick-tier rule. The `tick` migration track, `MT_TICK_*` settings and `mt data tick` namespace keep their names, so existing surfaces do not move (220, Technical Decision 3); a rename is out of scope.
- **Contract identity needs definitions for the same range** — Bar and statistics records carry only an instrument identifier, and an identifier is mapped to a contract through `tick_definition` validity windows. So `definition` records covering a range are a prerequisite of bar and statistics ingest for that range and of any roll rule that resolves contracts there. They are acquired with whatever range is bought, not ahead of it.
- **Manifest scale** — The manifest unit is one UTC day of a request. Full history of `ohlcv-1m`, `statistics` and `definition` for two products, if it is ever wanted, is on the order of tens of thousands of units and files, about a hundred times today's, and more with `ohlcv-1s`. Per-unit timings were measured in 226; manifest-scale queries, preflight and status over that many rows have not been, and slice 305 measured them.

## Anticipated Slices

In rough build order. The only money spent is the $0.06 statistics window slice 303 bought to evaluate the open-interest rule (303 D11).

- **Archive generalisation** — Schema families, per-schema projector registry, family-scoped supersession, and the plan-inclusion marker with its configuration table and guard change. Everything below builds on it.
- **`ohlcv-1m` with definition history** — The schema, its table, `definition` history wants, contract identity, and completeness for the tier (load-complete and the volume checks that need only `ohlcv-1m`), built on DBN sample files.
- **`statistics` and the open-interest roll rule** — The schema, session assignment and revision rules, completeness for the tier, the cross-tier volume check against `ohlcv-1m`, and the rule evaluated against calendar and volume for ES and GC.
- **Purchase path** — Spend limits, reconciliation and the purchase runner (304). Built, never run in production.
- **`ohlcv-1s`** — The schema and its completeness, when the PM names a use.
- **Futures completeness surfaces** — Tick, bar and statistics verdicts together in `health`, `accounting`, `overview` and the status coverage aggregates, and the manifest-scale measurement.
- **Time bars from held bars and ticks** — The source rule above: stored provider bars where held, ticks everywhere else, any time size, the source named, every applied parameter echoed. It replaces slice 306's provider-only serving and keeps its session-anchored buckets and source label. Open interest and settlement routes stay drafted in issue #73.
- **Tick bars and compact tick serving** — The incremental rollup module and its real-session fixtures, a tick-bar route with free N, and the compact raw-tick encoding (Technical Considerations, "Tick bars and raw ticks").
